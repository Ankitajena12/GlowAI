SKINCARE KNOWLEDGE BASE — SKIN TYPES & IDENTIFICATION

== HOW TO FIND YOUR SKIN TYPE ==
The Bare-Face Test:
1. Wash your face with a gentle cleanser
2. Pat dry and wait 30 minutes, don't apply anything
3. Observe your skin

Results:
- Shiny all over, visible pores, makeup slides off quickly → OILY skin
- Tight, flaky, rough patches, dull look → DRY skin
- Oily forehead, nose and chin (T-zone) but normal or dry cheeks → COMBINATION skin
- Redness, stinging, burning, reacts to most products → SENSITIVE skin
- Balanced, no shine, no tightness, minimal concerns → NORMAL skin

You can also dab blotting paper on nose and forehead after 1 hour. Lots of oil = oily/combination skin.

== OILY SKIN ==
Characteristics: Excess sebum, shiny appearance, enlarged pores, prone to blackheads and acne, makeup doesn't last.
Causes: Genetics, hormones, humidity, over-stripping skin (which triggers more oil production).
Best ingredients: Niacinamide, Salicylic acid (BHA), Zinc, Hyaluronic acid (lightweight), Kaolin clay.
Avoid: Heavy oils, thick creams, alcohol-based toners (dry out then rebound oilier), pore-clogging silicones.
Tip: Skipping moisturizer makes oily skin worse. Dehydrated oily skin produces more sebum to compensate.

Morning routine for oily skin:
1. Gentle gel or foaming cleanser
2. Alcohol-free toner with niacinamide or witch hazel
3. Lightweight niacinamide serum
4. Oil-free gel moisturizer
5. Broad-spectrum SPF 50 fluid or gel sunscreen

Evening routine for oily skin:
1. Double cleanse: micellar water then gel cleanser
2. BHA toner or serum (salicylic acid 0.5–2%), 2–3 times per week
3. Niacinamide 10% serum
4. Lightweight water-cream moisturizer

== DRY SKIN ==
Characteristics: Tight feeling, flaking, rough texture, dull complexion, fine lines more visible, can feel itchy.
Causes: Genetics, cold weather, low humidity, harsh cleansers, hot water, aging.
Best ingredients: Hyaluronic acid, Ceramides, Squalane, Glycerin, Shea butter, Lactic acid.
Avoid: Foaming cleansers, alcohol, hot water, physical scrubs, over-exfoliation.

Morning routine for dry skin:
1. Cream or oil cleanser
2. Hydrating toner or rose water mist
3. Vitamin C serum
4. Rich ceramide moisturizer
5. Cream-based SPF 50

Evening routine for dry skin:
1. Oil or balm cleanser
2. AHA (lactic acid) exfoliant 1–2 times per week
3. Hyaluronic acid serum on damp skin
4. Face oil (rosehip, squalane, or jojoba)
5. Rich night cream or sleeping mask

== COMBINATION SKIN ==
Characteristics: Oily T-zone (forehead, nose, chin) with normal or dry cheeks. Mixed pore size.
Strategy: Use lightweight products everywhere, slightly richer on dry zones. Consider zone-specific treatment.

Morning routine for combination skin:
1. Gentle gel or milk cleanser
2. Niacinamide toner
3. Hyaluronic acid serum (lightweight)
4. Gel-cream moisturizer
5. SPF fluid formula

Evening routine for combination skin:
1. Double cleanse
2. BHA on T-zone 2 times per week
3. AHA on dry cheek areas 1–2 times per week
4. Niacinamide serum all over
5. Light moisturizer on T-zone, richer cream on cheeks

Multi-masking tip: Apply clay mask on T-zone and hydrating mask on cheeks simultaneously.

== SENSITIVE SKIN ==
Characteristics: Easily irritated, redness, stinging, burning, reacts to fragrance and many actives.
Golden rule: Less is more. Introduce one new product every 2–4 weeks maximum.
Best ingredients: Centella asiatica, Ceramides, Allantoin, Panthenol, Bisabolol, Zinc oxide, Madecassoside.
Always avoid: Fragrances, essential oils, denatured alcohol, physical scrubs, harsh acids.

Morning routine for sensitive skin:
1. Micellar water or very gentle cream cleanser
2. Soothing centella or chamomile toner
3. Barrier repair serum with ceramides and peptides
4. Fragrance-free moisturizer
5. Mineral SPF (zinc oxide or titanium dioxide — gentlest option)

Evening routine for sensitive skin:
1. Gentle single cleanse
2. Centella asiatica or madecassoside serum
3. Rich fragrance-free ceramide moisturizer

Patch test every new product on inner arm for 24–48 hours before applying to face.
