SKINCARE KNOWLEDGE BASE — COMMON SKIN CONCERNS

== ACNE ==
What is acne: A skin condition where hair follicles become clogged with oil and dead skin cells causing whiteheads, blackheads, papules, pustules, nodules, or cysts.
Causes: Excess sebum, dead skin cells blocking pores, Cutibacterium acnes bacteria, inflammation, hormonal changes (puberty, menstrual cycle, PCOS), diet, stress, dirty pillowcases, touching face.

Types of acne:
- Non-inflammatory: Blackheads (open comedones), Whiteheads (closed comedones)
- Inflammatory: Papules (small red bumps), Pustules (pus-filled), Nodules (large, deep, painful), Cysts (deep, painful, pus-filled — most severe)

Routine for acne-prone skin:
1. Gentle non-foaming or low-lather cleanser
2. BHA toner (salicylic acid 1–2%) 3 times per week
3. Niacinamide 10% serum daily
4. Oil-free non-comedogenic moisturizer
5. SPF 30–50 fluid (essential — UV makes acne marks worse)

Spot treatments: Benzoyl peroxide 2.5% (kills bacteria), Salicylic acid patch overnight, Tea tree oil diluted 1:9 with carrier oil.

Diet and acne: High glycemic foods (white bread, sugar, white rice), dairy (especially skim milk) linked to worsened acne in some people. Zinc-rich foods (pumpkin seeds, lentils) may help.

What NOT to do: Squeeze or pop pimples (causes scarring and spreading), use toothpaste as spot treatment (causes more irritation and PIH), use very hot water, over-exfoliate.

For severe cystic or nodular acne: Always consult a dermatologist. Prescription options include topical tretinoin, clindamycin, oral antibiotics, hormonal therapy, or isotretinoin (Accutane).

== HYPERPIGMENTATION AND DARK SPOTS ==
What causes it: Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH) from acne, sun damage, melasma (hormonal, common in women), friction, aging.

Indian and darker skin tones are more prone to PIH and should choose gentler acids (mandelic or lactic over glycolic).

Top brightening ingredients:
1. Vitamin C — antioxidant, brightens, boosts collagen
2. Niacinamide — blocks melanin transfer, fades marks
3. Alpha Arbutin — gentle melanin inhibitor
4. Kojic acid — effective for melasma
5. Tranexamic acid — especially for melasma
6. AHAs (glycolic, lactic, mandelic) — exfoliate dark surface cells
7. Retinol — speeds cell turnover

SPF is the most important dark spot treatment: UV exposure makes all dark spots 10 times worse and prevents any serum from working. Use SPF 50 every single day.

Melasma: Hormonal dark patches usually on cheeks, forehead, upper lip. Triggered by sun, hormones, birth control. Very stubborn — requires consistent SPF + brightening ingredients + possibly prescription azelaic acid or hydroquinone from dermatologist.

Timeline: 4–12 weeks of consistent use to see improvement. Be patient.

== ANTI-AGING ==
Key causes of skin aging: UV exposure (80% of visible aging), loss of collagen and elastin, reduced cell turnover, repeated facial movements, smoking, sugar consumption, pollution.

Most evidence-backed anti-aging ingredients:
1. Retinol or retinoids (gold standard) — increases cell turnover, boosts collagen, fades spots, smooths texture
2. Vitamin C — antioxidant protection, collagen support, brightening
3. SPF 50 daily — prevents further photoaging
4. AHAs (glycolic acid) — exfoliates and smooths fine lines
5. Peptides — signal collagen production, great for retinol-sensitive skin
6. Hyaluronic acid — plumps and hydrates

Anti-aging morning routine: Vitamin C serum → Moisturizer → SPF 50
Anti-aging evening routine: Gentle cleanse → Retinol (2–3 nights per week to start) → Rich moisturizer

Lifestyle factors: Quality sleep 7–9 hours, no smoking, limit alcohol, antioxidant-rich diet, manage stress, wear sunglasses and hats outdoors.

== PORES AND BLACKHEADS ==
Truth about pores: Pore size is largely genetic. You cannot permanently shrink pores but you CAN minimize their appearance with consistent skincare.

Best products for pores:
- Niacinamide 5–10% — reduces pore appearance over weeks of use
- Salicylic acid (BHA) — dissolves oil and dead cells inside pores
- Clay masks (kaolin or bentonite) — temporarily draws out impurities
- Retinol — long-term pore refinement with regular use

Blackheads: Caused by oxidized oil and dead skin in open pores (not dirt). BHA is the best treatment.
Avoid pore strips (damage pore walls, making them look larger long-term). Never squeeze.

== ROSACEA ==
Characteristics: Chronic redness, visible blood vessels, flushing, sometimes acne-like bumps. Common triggers include sun, heat, spicy food, alcohol, stress.
Skincare approach: Ultra gentle routine. Mineral SPF. Fragrance-free everything. Centella asiatica, azelaic acid, green tea extract soothe redness.
Always see a dermatologist for rosacea diagnosis and treatment — topical metronidazole, azelaic acid, ivermectin cream are common prescriptions.

== ECZEMA AND DRY SKIN CONDITIONS ==
Characteristics: Itchy, inflamed, cracked skin. Compromised skin barrier lets moisture escape and irritants in.
Treatment: Fragrance-free products only. Thick moisturizers (creams or ointments) with ceramides. Colloidal oatmeal soothes. Petroleum jelly as an occlusive.
Avoid: Fragrances, preservatives like methylisothiazolinone, foaming cleansers, hot water, wool fabric.
Consult a dermatologist for prescription steroid creams if over-the-counter options fail.

== UNDER-EYE DARK CIRCLES ==
Causes: Genetics and thin skin showing blood vessels (bluish), melanin pigmentation (brownish), lack of sleep, dehydration, allergies.
Treatments: Caffeine (reduces puffiness and constricts vessels), Vitamin K, Peptides (thickens skin over time), Vitamin C (brightens pigmented circles), Retinol (gentle eye-safe formulas).
Quick fixes: Cold spoons or cool eye mask in morning, sleep with head slightly elevated, stay hydrated.
Note: Genetic dark circles cannot be completely eliminated with skincare alone — cosmetic treatments or concealer may be needed.

== SUNBURN ==
Immediate care: Cool (not cold) water, aloe vera gel, fragrance-free gentle moisturizer.
Avoid: Further sun exposure, anything with alcohol or fragrance, exfoliants, retinol until healed.
Do not pop any blisters — risk of infection.
Severe sunburn with blistering, fever, or large area: Seek medical attention.

== FUNGAL ACNE (Malassezia folliculitis) ==
Often mistaken for regular acne. Caused by Malassezia yeast overgrowth.
Characteristics: Small uniform itchy bumps, usually on forehead, chest, back.
Treatment: Antifungal (ketoconazole shampoo used as face wash, zinc pyrithione). Avoid fatty acids in products (certain oils feed Malassezia).
Spike in humid weather and after sweating — common in India during monsoon.
Consult a dermatologist to confirm diagnosis.
