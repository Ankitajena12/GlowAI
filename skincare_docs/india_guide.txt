SKINCARE KNOWLEDGE BASE — INDIA SPECIFIC GUIDE

== INDIAN AND SOUTH ASIAN SKIN ==
Indian skin has higher melanin content providing natural SPF and slower visible aging. However it comes with specific concerns.

Common concerns for Indian skin:
- Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH): Dark marks left after acne, cuts, or any injury. Higher melanin means marks are more visible and take longer to fade.
- Melasma: Hormonal dark patches on cheeks, forehead, upper lip. Very common in Indian women especially during pregnancy or on oral contraceptives.
- Oiliness and combination skin: Hot humid climate triggers more sebum production.
- Uneven skin tone and sun tan: Year-round intense UV exposure in India.
- Acne: Combination of heat, humidity, oil, and diet factors.

Key advice for Indian skin:
- SPF is the single most important product. UV index in India is extreme year-round. Use SPF 50 with PA+++ or PA++++ rating. Reapply every 2 hours outdoors.
- Be gentle with acid exfoliants. Glycolic acid at high concentrations can trigger PIH in darker skin. Prefer lactic acid or mandelic acid which are gentler.
- Avoid harsh whitening and bleaching products. Many over-the-counter fairness creams in India contain harmful steroids, mercury, or hydroquinone at unsafe concentrations.
- For brightening: Niacinamide, Alpha arbutin, Vitamin C, Kojic acid, Tranexamic acid are safe and effective.
- Healthy skin — not lighter skin — is the goal. Embrace your natural skin tone.

== SUNSCREEN FOR INDIAN SKIN ==
Why SPF is essential: India receives intense UV radiation year-round. UV causes dark spots, aging, uneven tone.
Recommended: SPF 50, Broad spectrum, PA+++ or PA++++

Types of sunscreen suitable for Indian skin:
- Gel or fluid sunscreens: No white cast, great for oily and combination skin.
- Cream sunscreens: Better for dry skin.
- Mineral sunscreens (zinc oxide): Safest for sensitive skin, may leave white cast on deeper skin tones — look for tinted versions.
- Chemical sunscreens: No white cast, more cosmetically elegant — use if no sensitivity.

Application: Apply as last step in morning routine. Use a quarter teaspoon for face alone. Reapply every 2 hours outdoors.

Budget sunscreen options in India:
- Minimalist SPF 50 PA++++ (excellent, no white cast)
- Neutrogena Ultra Sheer SPF 50+
- Lakme Sun Expert SPF 50
- Lotus Herbals Safe Sun SPF 50
- Dot and Key Mattifying SPF 50

== CLIMATE AND SEASONS IN INDIA ==
Summer (March to June) — hot and humid especially in Odisha, Bengal, Maharashtra:
- Switch to gel or water-based moisturizer
- Reapply SPF every 2 hours when outdoors
- Double cleanse evening to remove sweat and sunscreen
- Use hydrating mist (rose water) to cool and refresh skin
- Blotting papers instead of reapplying powder

Monsoon (June to September) — humid:
- Skin feels stickier than usual
- BHA (salicylic acid) helps prevent fungal acne which spikes in humidity
- Keep routine minimal — humidity itself provides moisture
- Cleanse well after sweating

Winter (November to February) — drier especially in North India:
- Switch to cream or balm moisturizer
- Add face oil as last step in evening to seal moisture
- Use a humidifier in bedroom if possible
- Lukewarm water only — hot water strips skin barrier

== HOME REMEDIES — WHAT WORKS AND WHAT DOES NOT ==
SAFE and evidence-backed:
- Aloe vera gel: Soothes redness, hydrates, helps with mild sunburn.
- Raw honey: Antibacterial humectant, good as a 10-minute mask.
- Colloidal oatmeal: Calms eczema and irritation, used in many pharmacy products.
- Green tea (cooled): Antioxidant toner, reduces undereye puffiness as compress.
- Ice cube wrapped in cloth: Reduces redness and puffiness temporarily.

Use with CAUTION:
- Turmeric: Can stain skin yellow, may irritate; always dilute and patch test.
- Multani mitti (Fuller's earth): Fine as 1–2 times weekly mask for oily skin, not daily.

DO NOT apply to face:
- Lemon juice: pH 2, causes burns and severe PIH especially on Indian skin. Never use directly.
- Baking soda: Destroys skin barrier (pH 9), causes damage.
- Toothpaste on pimples: Contains sulfates and menthol that cause irritation and dark marks.
- Coconut oil on face: Very comedogenic, blocks pores and causes breakouts for most skin types.
- Apple cider vinegar: Can cause chemical burns undiluted.

When to see a dermatologist:
- Severe or cystic acne
- Sudden rash, hives, or allergic reaction
- Unexplained skin darkening or rapid changes
- Persistent eczema or psoriasis
- Any suspicious mole or skin growth
- Skin that burns, bleeds, or does not heal
- Melasma that does not respond to over-the-counter treatment
